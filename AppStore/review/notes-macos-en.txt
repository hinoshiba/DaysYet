DaysYet for Mac is a native app for macOS 14 or later. No account or sign-in is required. It is not a WidgetKit extension and is not added through the macOS widget gallery.

Resident panel and customization:
1. Launch DaysYet. Three circles appear at the right edge for this week, month, and year, with no personal dates required. Hover to reveal a timeline's details. Double-click the panel, circle, or detail surface to open settings; a single click does not open settings. There is no menu bar item or custom right-click menu.
2. In Settings > Timelines > Your timelines, use Add Circle to show 3–8 distinct timelines; remove controls appear above the minimum of three. Choosing a timeline already displayed swaps its position. Left/right placement shows all selected circles. Top placement uses only the first three, keeping later selections saved.
3. Drag a side panel vertically from its black surface, circle, or open detail. Release to save the position for the next launch. Dragging holds the detail state and does not open settings. Settings offer a Center button; there is no vertical-position slider. Top placement stays centered and does not drag. Choose a display, left/right/top edge, and size from 80–150% in Settings > Widget.
4. Under Colors in the Widget tab, select one of four templates or click a visible timeline's color to customize it. Colors update and save immediately. Reset one color or all colors to the template. Choosing a template replaces all custom colors. Top placement shows color controls for the first three timelines.
5. Enable Launch at login at the top of the Widget tab to start DaysYet when signing in to this Mac. It is user-controlled. If macOS approval is required, use Open Login Items Settings to enable it there; return to DaysYet to refresh the status.
6. Detail modes are Countdown, Time + elapsed % + bar, and Progress bars. In Progress bars, select time left, elapsed progress, or end date as the value. Hover over a top bar or just above it to select a timeline; higher in the camera area, hover opens the current one. Moving away closes details unless Keep details open is enabled. Settings also provide Show/hide and Quit. Relaunching a hidden app opens settings, where you can enable visibility. Closing settings leaves the panel running.

Study days (new in 0.1.5):
1. Open Settings > Timelines > Choose Study Days. Set a name and inclusive date range of up to 366 days; two-week and four-week shortcuts are available.
2. Choose weekdays, then click dates to add or exclude them. Changing weekdays resets individual date adjustments, as explained in the editor. You can deselect all weekdays and choose dates individually. Changes save automatically.
3. Select Study days under Your timelines. Remaining days include today when selected; progress is past selected days divided by all selected days. This is a planned-day countdown, not completed study tracking. Empty and elapsed plans have distinct states. The count follows the local date; no Mac calendar access or permission is used.

In Timelines, two named activity schedules support start/end times and active weekdays. Defaults are Daily activity 07:00–23:00 every day and Work hours 09:00–18:00 Monday–Friday. Earlier end times finish the next day even if that day is Off; the start day controls whether a schedule runs. Equal times define 24 hours; all weekdays can be Off. Existing work times and selections are preserved. Week starts on uses the device calendar or your chosen weekday.

Dates, goals, activities, study plans, and panel preferences stay in this Mac's sandbox, separate from iPhone/iPad with no sync. Delete all data resets saved entries and panel preferences. No application-initiated network requests, analytics, ads, or third-party runtime SDKs are used. Public links open a browser after user action. The healthy-age goal is a planning marker, not medical advice or a health/lifespan prediction. Activities do not track attendance or report to employers.
