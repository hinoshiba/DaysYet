Frequently asked questions

Get started on Mac

The Mac app supports macOS 14 or later and is being prepared for the Mac App Store. Try the interactive preview on this page before release. In the Mac app, launching DaysYet shows this week, month, and year at the right edge of your display. The panel stays active when you close settings. Double-click the widget to open settings. There is no widget to add from the macOS widget gallery.

Change the Mac panel’s position or contents

Double-click the widget to open settings. Choose three timelines, the detail mode, the value in Progress bars mode (time left, elapsed progress, or end date), theme, display, left/right/top placement, vertical position for side placement, and size (80–150%, default 100%). Circles and text scale together. Hover over a timeline to open details. At either side, the expanded section and its details move up or down to align with the selected circle. At the top, hover over the bars or just above them to switch timelines. Higher in the camera area, hovering opens the current timeline. Both the band and line thickness follow the size setting. Double-click the widget to open settings, including the option to keep details open. Move the pointer away to close details. If Keep details open is enabled, turn it off in settings to close them. Settings include visibility and Quit controls. If the widget is hidden, open DaysYet again to access settings and show it.

The Mac panel is missing

Open DaysYet again to access settings and enable the panel. Review the selected display and position in settings. If the problem continues, contact support with your Mac and macOS version, connected displays, and reproduction steps.

Change the first day of the week on Mac

In the upcoming Mac app, open Settings → Timelines → Week starts on. The default follows your device’s calendar setting and shows the weekday currently in use. You can choose any weekday instead, measuring This week from local midnight on that day to local midnight on the same day the following week. The choice updates time left, elapsed progress, and the end date in the Mac app.

Set a daily work countdown on Mac

In the upcoming Mac app, set your start and end times, then choose Work hours as a displayed timeline. The default is 9:00 AM–6:00 PM, repeating every day in the device’s local time. For daytime shifts, it indicates when work has not started, shows time left during work, and marks work as finished afterward. Overnight shifts such as 10:00 PM–6:00 AM are supported; matching start and end times mean a 24-hour shift. Work times stay on the device and do not sync elsewhere.

Sync between iPhone, iPad, and Mac

There is no device sync. Set up timelines and personal goals separately on each device. Deleting data affects only the device where you perform the action.

Add the widget

On the Home Screen, touch and hold an empty area, choose Edit, search for DaysYet, and add Small or Medium. On the Lock Screen, touch and hold the screen, choose Customize, and tap a Lock Screen widget area to add DaysYet. Choose Inline, Circular, or Rectangular.

Change the displayed timelines

Select three timelines on the app’s Widget tab for the Home Screen. To override one Home Screen widget, touch and hold it, choose Edit Widget, disable “Use the three selected in the app,” and select its timelines. To choose the one timeline shown on the Lock Screen, tap the widget while customizing the Lock Screen.

Change the display mode or theme

For the Home Screen, use the app’s Widget tab to choose Time, Time + %, or Bars and select one of four themes. Time + % shows time left, elapsed percentage, and a progress bar that fills from left to right in every row. To give one widget a different display or color, open Edit Widget from the Home Screen and override the app setting.

Change a milestone’s start date

Open Edit life reference points from the Times tab, then set the milestone’s start and target dates. Progress starts at 0% on the start date and reaches 100% at the target.

The widget is not updating

Open DaysYet once and save the settings again, then confirm the widget remains on the Home Screen or Lock Screen. The operating system schedules widget refreshes, so values do not update every second. If the problem continues, email support@hinoshiba.com with the device, OS, and app version.

Delete data

Choose “Delete all data” from the Settings tab on iPhone or iPad, or from settings opened by double-clicking the Mac widget. On Mac, panel layout and size preferences also return to their defaults. Other devices are unaffected. Data entered in DaysYet is not stored on a developer server.

Privacy · Last updated 2026-09-06

Your entries stay
on this device.

In short: DaysYet does not collect or transmit user data and contains no tracking, advertising, analytics, or third-party SDKs. Your entries stay on device. This policy also covers the Mac app being prepared for release.

You can review the privacy summary in Settings and delete all saved data at any time.

1. Data stored and purpose

DaysYet stores the birth date, healthy-age goal, milestone name, start and target dates, daily work start and end times, week-start preference, widget selections, display mode, value-display style, and theme. Showing Work hours is optional; work times are used for a daily countdown based on the device’s local time. The Mac app also stores panel visibility, selected display, screen edge, vertical position, size including circles and text, and the preference to keep details open. Data is used only to calculate and display timelines in the app, widget, and desktop panel.

2. Storage and sharing

On iPhone and iPad, data stays in an on-device App Group container shared by the app and its Widget Extension. On Mac, it stays in the Mac app’s own sandbox. Data does not sync between devices. It is not sent to the developer, a server, cloud service, analytics or advertising provider, or any other third party. DaysYet does not create accounts.

A Home Screen widget, Lock Screen widget, or Mac desktop panel may be visible to anyone who can view the device. A Lock Screen widget may remain visible while the device is locked.

3. Tracking, advertising, and analytics

DaysYet does not track users, show advertising, perform behavioral analytics, or include third-party SDKs. Apple may process App Store and operating-system information under Apple’s own privacy policy.

4. Health-related information

The healthy-age goal is a personal planning marker chosen by the user. DaysYet does not access HealthKit and does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, health measurement, treatment, or a lifespan prediction. No external health-statistics dataset is bundled.

5. Deletion and retention

“Delete all data” in Settings immediately removes saved data on the current device. On Mac it also resets panel layout and size preferences. Other devices are unaffected. Any on-device data remaining after app deletion is managed by the operating system. Because the developer does not receive or retain app data, there is no developer-side app record to delete.

6. External links and changes

The system browser opens only when the user taps a source-code, Privacy, or Support link. Before adding networking, cloud sync, crash analytics, server-side purchase validation, HealthKit, or a third-party SDK, the project will update this policy, its Privacy Manifests, and App Store privacy answers.

7. Information shared when contacting us

Contact us at support@hinoshiba.com. If you choose to email us, your sender address and message are used to reply and provide necessary support. Do not send personal dates, health information, credentials, or private keys. To report a vulnerability, do not include details in the email; ask for a secure reporting channel.
